Privacy policy

Vitsy · Last updated 3 November 2026

The short version: what you enter in Vitsy stays on your phone, in an encrypted database. There's no account, no ads in the app and no tracking, and we don't receive your records.

Who we are

Vitsy is made by Neutrade Ltd, which is responsible for this policy. You can reach us at support@neutrade.co.uk.

What the app stores

Everything you enter is kept on your phone: the supplements and medicines you track, your stacks, schedules and reminders, what you log, your training, your Diary check-ins (mood, energy, tags and notes) and your settings. It's held in an encrypted database, and the key to it stays on that phone.

You don't need an account. Sign-in, Friends and cloud backup are not part of this version of Vitsy. If we add them, we'll update this policy before they're available.

Phone backups

Vitsy's database is left out of your iPhone's iCloud and computer backups, and out of Android's automatic backup. That means a new or restored phone won't bring your Vitsy records with it. To keep a copy, use Export your data in Settings.

What the app sends

Nothing from your records. Vitsy has no ads in the app, no analytics and no tracking.

Vitsy asks this website, vitsy.app, for a small settings file that tells it whether to show a feature. It asks a moment after it starts, then at most about once an hour while the app is open. The request carries nothing about you, your records or your phone's identity: no account, no device identifier and no cookies. The website is hosted by GitHub Pages, so GitHub can see your phone's IP address and the basic technical details any web request carries, as it does for visits to the website.

If this version of Vitsy has crash reporting turned on, it may send Sentry, a crash-reporting service, a short note each time the app opens and closes, so we can count how many sessions end without a crash. It may also send a report when the app crashes or hits an error. Sentry uses its EU data region and, like any service your phone contacts, sees your phone's IP address. Notes and reports hold technical details only: the kind of error, where in the app it happened, the app version, the operating system, the device family and a random number for this install. They're built from a fixed list of allowed fields, so names, notes and what you take are never included.

Each time it starts, Vitsy also checks with RevenueCat, the company that manages Plus subscriptions for us, whether you have Plus, and on iPhone passes it Apple's ad attribution token. What it sends is set out under Payments and Plus below.

Separately, if you have turned on Apple's “Share With App Developers” or Android's equivalent, Apple or Google may send us crash reports. Those come from them, not from Vitsy.

Apart from these, the app sends nothing.

Apple Health

On iPhone, Vitsy can read from Apple Health, only if you allow it. It reads your workouts and your sleep. On iOS 26 or later, if you choose to import medications, it reads only the ones you pick in Apple's own screen. It never writes anything to Health.

Workouts it reads are used for Training and for timing around workouts, and are kept with your training history on your phone. Sleep is shown in the Diary while you look at it and is never stored, logged, exported or backed up by Vitsy. Imported medications become items in Vitsy only after you confirm them. From then on they're kept on your phone like any other item you add.

You can turn access off at any time in the Health app or in your iPhone's Settings.

Reminders and notifications

Reminders, including the check-in reminder, are scheduled on your phone, not sent from a server. By default, item names stay off your lock screen: notifications show the stack name, such as “Morning”. In Settings, under Names outside the app, you can show names or hide them all.

The same setting covers widgets, the Apple Watch app and Siri. What they show is passed from your phone to your own devices, not through us.

If you turn on the check-in reminder, Vitsy uses whether you've already checked in that day to decide whether to schedule it. That check happens on your phone.

The Diary, Patterns and the support numbers

Check-ins are kept on your phone, are included when you export your data, and are removed by Delete all data. Patterns are worked out on your phone each time you look, and are never stored, sent or logged.

If several recent check-ins show a low mood, Vitsy may quietly offer support numbers, at most once a week: in the UK, Samaritans, NHS 111 and 999; in the US, 988 and 911; elsewhere, a link to findahelpline.com. This is decided on your phone and nothing is sent. Which ones you see depends on your phone's region and time zone, which Vitsy reads on your phone. The only thing Vitsy keeps about it is the date it was last shown, stored only on this phone and removed by Delete all data. You can turn it off in Settings, under Diary.

Payments and Plus

Plus is bought through the App Store or Google Play. They take the payment, and neither we nor RevenueCat see your card details. You manage or cancel your subscription in your App Store or Google Play account.

RevenueCat, Inc. manages Plus subscriptions for us. It acts as our processor, which means it uses this information only on our instructions: to check and manage your subscription, to restore your purchases on a new phone, and to tell us which of our App Store ads led to installs and subscriptions. Each time Vitsy starts, it sends RevenueCat a random app user ID that isn't linked to your name or email, your App Store or Google Play purchase receipts, and your phone's IP address. RevenueCat's software also sends Apple's identifier for vendor (a code Apple gives each app maker for your phone), your phone's model, its operating system version, your language settings, your App Store country and the Vitsy version. RevenueCat also records whether apps are allowed to track you on this phone; Vitsy doesn't ask to track you and doesn't track you. Vitsy never sends your records, item names or health information to RevenueCat, and you don't need an account.

On iPhone, Vitsy also passes RevenueCat a one-off token from Apple's ad attribution service. RevenueCat uses it to ask Apple whether the install came from one of our ads in the App Store and, if it did, which campaign, ad group and keyword. It records that with your subscription, so we can see which ads lead to trials and subscriptions. The token holds no name, email or advertising identifier, and Vitsy doesn't ask to track you.

RevenueCat is based in the United States, so this information goes there. The transfer is covered by the International Data Transfer Addendum issued by the UK Information Commissioner, added to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, as set out in RevenueCat's data processing terms.

Exports and diagnostics

When you export your data, the file is made on your phone. Once you share or move it, it's outside the app and you control where it goes.

If you contact support from the app, Vitsy opens a ready-written email in your own email app, and you choose whether to send it. You can add a diagnostics file if you want to. You see it before it's added. It holds technical details such as versions, settings and reminder times, and leaves out item names unless you tick the box to include them.

If your records won't open, the app offers a ready-written email to us in the same way. It holds only a reason code and the app version.

When you email us

If you email us, including to register interest, we receive your email address and what you write. We use them only to answer you, or to tell you when Vitsy launches if that's what you asked. If your message includes health information, we use it only to help you.

This website

This website sets no cookies and has no analytics. Its fonts and images are served from the site itself, so no other company sees your visit through them. The site is hosted by GitHub Pages, and GitHub may record visitors' IP addresses for security.

Age

Vitsy is for adults. You need to be 18 or over to use it.

Your choices and rights

Because your records are on your phone, you control them directly: export them or delete them in Settings at any time. Deleting the app also removes them. For anything we hold, such as your emails, you can ask us for a copy or ask us to delete it. If you're unhappy with how we've handled your information, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and the date at the top.
